The [LICENSE](LICENSE) lets you use, modify and share this project **free of
charge for education, teaching and non-commercial research**, as long as you
credit the author. It does **not** let you make money with it.

If money is involved anywhere in your use, you need a written **Commercial
Licence** from the author first. This page explains when, how, and on what
terms.

## Do I need one?

**You do NOT need a commercial licence if:**

- you are a student, teacher, researcher or academic using it for coursework,
  teaching, a thesis, a paper or academic research;
- you work at a university, school, research institute, national lab, public
  library or museum and are using it for that institution's educational or
  academic mission;
- you are learning, tinkering, or evaluating it on your own time;
- you run a free public instance that credits the author and charges nobody
  anything, directly or indirectly.

**You DO need one if:**

| Situation | Needs a licence |
|---|---|
| Selling it, or a product containing it | Yes |
| Hosting it as a paid, freemium, ad-supported or subscription service | Yes |
| Using it inside a for-profit company to design or validate anything you sell | Yes |
| Paid consulting, paid training, paid workshops, contract research for a fee | Yes |
| Bundling it into a commercial product, appliance or distribution | Yes |
| Monetised content (ads, sponsorship, paid media) produced with it | Yes |
| University research funded by an industrial sponsor, results owned by the sponsor | Ask — usually yes |
| University spin-out that has started selling | Yes, at reduced terms |
| Internal R&D at a company, nothing shipped yet | Ask — often a free evaluation licence |

When in doubt, ask. Asking costs nothing and takes one email.

## How to request one

Email **shuvam@sycolex.com** with the subject line:

```
Commercial licence request — lammps-web-gui
```

Include:

1. **Who you are** — legal entity name, country, website, your role.
2. **What you are building** — the product, service or deliverable.
3. **How this project is used** — embedded? hosted? internal tooling? which
   parts (script builder, compiler helper, viewer, parsers)?
4. **Scale** — expected users, seats, instances or engagements per year.
5. **Your pricing model** — how *you* charge, and your expected revenue from
   the offering in the first 12 months.
6. **Timeline** — when you intend to launch.
7. **Attribution plan** — where the credit required by LICENSE §3.2 will
   appear in your UI and docs.

Expect a first response within about two weeks. A request is not permission —
**only a signed agreement is.** Silence is not consent (LICENSE §5.1).

## Terms

The default headline terms, from LICENSE §5:

- **Royalty: 10% of Revenue.** Revenue is gross — before costs, taxes,
  commissions, refunds and discounts — for any product, service or engagement
  that uses, embeds, derives from, or was produced with the help of this
  software.
- **Apportionment.** If this software is one component of a larger offering,
  we agree a fair apportionment *in writing before the licence starts*. With
  no written apportionment, the royalty applies to the whole offering.
- **Reporting.** A written revenue statement and payment within 30 days of
  each calendar quarter's end. Late amounts accrue 1.5%/month.
- **Audit.** Once a year, 30 days' notice, confidential. If an audit finds an
  underpayment over 5%, you pay for the audit as well as the shortfall.
- **Attribution still applies.** A commercial licence does not remove the
  attribution requirements in LICENSE §3.

### These are negotiable

The 10% is a starting point, not a wall. Reduced, deferred or waived terms are
genuinely available for:

- academic spin-offs and university technology-transfer companies,
- registered non-profits and NGOs,
- independent developers and small teams,
- educational publishers and course platforms giving the tool away free,
- anyone contributing substantial improvements back upstream.

Say so in your email and make the case.
